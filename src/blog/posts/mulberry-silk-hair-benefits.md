---

## What Is Momme Weight?

Momme (pronounced "mummy," abbreviated mm) is the traditional unit of measurement for silk fabric weight. Specifically, it measures the weight in pounds of a piece of silk that is 45 inches wide and 100 yards long.

The higher the momme number:
- The denser and heavier the fabric
- The more silk threads per square inch
- The more durable, opaque, and beneficial the material
- The higher the cost of production

Common silk weights by use:
- **8–12mm:** Very lightweight, sheer; used in scarves and linings
- **16–19mm:** Medium weight; entry-level "silk" pillowcases and accessories
- **22mm:** Premium weight; the benchmark for quality bedding and professional hair accessories
- **25mm+:** Extremely heavy; upholstery and specialty applications

For hair care, 22mm is the meaningful threshold.

## What Is Mulberry Silk — and Why Does the Source Matter?

Not all silk comes from the same silkworm or the same process. The term "mulberry silk" refers specifically to silk produced by Bombyx mori silkworms that are raised on a controlled diet of white mulberry leaves.

This diet consistency produces several distinguishing qualities:
- **Uniformly fine filaments:** Mulberry silk filaments are longer and more consistent in diameter than those from wild silkworms (tasar, eri, muga), resulting in a smoother, more uniform fabric with less variation in surface texture.
- **High luster:** The near-perfect cylindrical structure of mulberry silk filaments reflects light uniformly, creating that signature luminous sheen.
- **Exceptional softness:** The fine, consistent filaments lie flat against each other, creating a naturally smooth hand feel that doesn't roughen over time the way coarser silks can.
- **Structural integrity:** Mulberry silk has a naturally high tensile strength relative to its weight — it doesn't easily snag, pill, or degrade with proper care.

Compared to polyester satin or lower-grade silk blends: there is no comparison in performance over time.

## How 22 Momme Mulberry Silk Affects Your Hair

### Friction Reduction

Hair damage from tools, pillowcases, and accessories is largely mechanical — caused by friction that roughens and lifts the cuticle layer. Lifted cuticles mean:
- Visible frizz
- Increased tangling
- Color fade (dye escapes through lifted cuticle scales)
- Moisture loss

At 22mm, mulberry silk has a friction coefficient low enough to allow hair to move against it without resistance. A 16mm silk or polyester satin will feel smooth but still generates enough friction over a full night's sleep to cause measurable cuticle disruption.

### Moisture Balance

Silk is a protein fiber — and one of its defining properties is a semi-permeable structure that doesn't aggressively absorb moisture from whatever it contacts.

For hair: when you sleep on or wrap your hair in 22mm mulberry silk, the moisture you applied through conditioning doesn't transfer to the fabric. Your hair wakes up with the moisture balance you set it with.

This is critical for:
- Color-treated hair (loses moisture more easily due to altered protein structure)
- Natural and curly hair (needs maintained moisture for curl pattern definition)
- Any hair type in dry climates or during winter months

### Thermoregulation

Silk's protein-based structure (fibroin and sericin) gives it natural thermoregulating properties. It stays cool in warm temperatures and retains warmth in cooler conditions.

For overnight hair care, this means your hair isn't being baked in retained heat throughout the night — a factor that contributes to frizz and cuticle disruption even without active heat tools.

### Hypoallergenic Properties

Sericin, the protein component that bonds silk filaments together, has natural hypoallergenic and antimicrobial properties. For sensitive scalps or those prone to product buildup, this is a meaningful benefit from the fabric you're using nightly.

## How to Evaluate Silk Quality When You Buy

1. **Always check for 100% mulberry silk or 100% pure silk.** Blended silks (silk + polyester) perform significantly worse.

2. **Look for the momme weight, explicitly stated.** If a brand doesn't list it, assume it's on the lower end or polyester.

3. **Check for OEKO-TEX Standard 100 certification** — this verifies that no harmful dyes or chemicals were used in processing. It's a sign that the brand takes material integrity seriously.

4. **Notice the price.** 22mm mulberry silk has a real production cost. If the price seems too good to be true, the fiber content probably isn't what it claims.

5. **Care instructions matter.** Genuine silk requires hand wash or gentle machine wash in a mesh bag, cold water, mild detergent. If a product labeled "silk" says machine wash warm or tumble dry — question it.
